# NIA Funding — Real Targets for a Grants-&-Sponsorships Model

*Researched 2026-08-10 against your "grants & sponsorships only" decision. Grouped by fit. The entity choice (LLC vs C-corp) gates the first category — read the partnership review alongside this.*

## The honest framing first
NIA is two things wearing one coat: a **media product** (Napkin + Intelligence Layer) and a **data/software product** (the Terminal). They qualify for *different* money. Chase both tracks; don't force one grant to cover both.

## Track 1 — SBIR/STTR (fits the Terminal, if you frame R&D)
Federal non-dilutive money, explicitly for-profit, explicitly **anti-VC** — which is exactly your model.

- **NSF SBIR Phase I** — up to **$305,000**, non-dilutive. Eligibility: US for-profit small business, ≥51% US-owned, no VC/PE majority (you qualify). Software/AI/"scalable analytics" is a funded topic. Process: a required **Project Pitch** (pitches open ~June 2, 2026; NSF replies in 1–2 months), then invited full proposal (deadlines Jul 27 & Nov 4, 2026). **Caveat that decides it:** SBIR funds *R&D on a technical innovation*, not "we built a newsletter." Your fundable angle is the hard part — cross-source entity resolution across patents/grants/trials/theses, or the ML that classifies and links neurotech signals — pitched as novel data-integration research. If you can't write the pitch around a genuine technical unknown, skip SBIR; don't contort a media product into it.
- **NIH SBIR/STTR (BRAIN Initiative / NINDS)** — larger awards, but the R&D must be biomedical. Only relevant if NIA spins out an actual analytical *tool for researchers* (e.g., a neurotech landscape/discovery instrument), not for the media product. Lower priority than NSF for what you're building.
- **Entity gate:** SBIR needs a for-profit; both LLC and C-corp qualify. This is another reason to settle entity structure now (partnership review) — the NSF Project Pitch window opens in weeks.

## Track 2 — Journalism/media grants (fit the Napkin + Intelligence Layer)
Your written products are independent trade journalism — a real funding category.

- **Lenfest Institute for Journalism** — grants for sustainable independent journalism and news-product innovation. Best fit for "data-driven independent industry intelligence." Check current cycles at lenfestinstitute.org/our-work/grants.
- **GFMD "Next Challenge for Media & Journalism 2026"** — up to **$250,000** for media/journalism ventures (verify eligibility/geography).
- **SPJ Foundation grants** and the journalism-grant databases (Instrumentl, Indiegraf's 2026 list) — smaller, but stackable and good for credibility.
- Most media grants prefer **nonprofit or fiscally-sponsored** applicants. A **fiscal sponsor** (a nonprofit that receives the grant on your behalf for a fee) lets a for-profit LLC access this money without converting — worth one call.

## Track 3 — Sponsorships (fastest, no entity gymnastics)
Your audience *is* the neurotech industry — sponsors want exactly these eyeballs. This is revenue you can land before any grant clears.

- **Newsletter/Terminal sponsorship:** neurotech-adjacent vendors — electrode/DAQ makers (g.tec, OpenBCI, Blackrock), CRO/regulatory-consulting firms, component suppliers, recruiters targeting neuro talent. One placement per issue; price on audience quality, not size (a 500-person all-industry list is worth more than 50k random readers).
- **Warm inroads you already have:** Neurotech@Berkeley's partners (DeepMind, Microsoft AI, Openwater, Power Kinetics) and Luc's Ampa network and Daniel's Somnee network are your first sponsor conversations — founders talking to adjacent companies.
- **Conference/data partnerships:** neurotech conferences and market-research firms may license or co-brand the Terminal's landscape data.

## Sequencing (match your Apr–Sep campaign, now in the Aug slot)
1. **Now:** open sponsorship conversations via the three founders' networks — this is money in weeks, no paperwork. One sponsor at ~$500–2k/issue underwrites the whole campaign budget's low end.
2. **Sept–Oct:** decide entity, then submit the **NSF Project Pitch** if the Terminal has a defensible R&D story.
3. **Q4:** one media-grant application (Lenfest or GFMD) via a fiscal sponsor if you stay LLC.
4. **Parallel bridge revenue:** the $500–2k one-off landscape reports (positioning doc) — sellable to the same companies you're pitching for sponsorship.

## Reality check
Grants are slow (3–9 months) and SBIR is uncertain (framing risk). Sponsorship + one-off reports are your near-term oxygen; grants are the scale-up layer. Your budget doc's "lean year hugs the low end (~$35k)" is very achievable on sponsorship + reports alone, before a single grant lands.
