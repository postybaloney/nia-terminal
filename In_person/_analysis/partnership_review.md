## The one thing to resolve before filing: LLC vs. C-corp
Your docs contain a genuine contradiction worth 30 minutes now instead of $5k later. You wrote "bootstrapped **LLC**," but you also designed **stock**, an **employee option pool**, and an **investor share class**, and noted "Use Stripe for incorporation" — Stripe Atlas creates a **Delaware C-corp**. Those are two different worlds:
- An **LLC** has membership units, not stock; no clean option pool; pass-through taxes; awkward for outside investors — but simple and cheap for a bootstrapped, grants-funded project.
- A **C-corp** is what your equity design (option pool, investor class, "stock distribution") actually describes, and what most grant programs and any future investor expect — but more formality and cost.
Your own note "grants and funding types sacrificed" shows you already sense the tradeoff. **Decide deliberately:** if it's truly grants + sponsorship only and stays small, an LLC with a members' operating agreement is fine — but then drop the stock/option-pool language and express the 85/15 split as membership units with a contributor unit pool. If you want an employee option pool and any investor path, incorporate as a Delaware C-corp from the start. Don't file an LLC and paper it like a C-corp.
