## 1 · The Signal

On **June 17, 2026**, Paradromics implanted its **Connexus** brain-computer interface in a human for the first time, at **University of Michigan Health**, in a patient with motor neuron disease and severe speech and motor impairment. The procedure opens the **Connect-One Early Feasibility Study**, which the FDA cleared under an Investigational Device Exemption on **November 20, 2025** and which will run across UC Davis, Massachusetts General Hospital, and Michigan ([Paradromics](https://paradromics.com/news/paradromics-completes-first-human-brain-computer-interface-bci-implantation/); [Michigan Medicine](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/news-release/university-michigan-implants-first-human-paradromics-wireless-brain-computer-interface-designed); [Paradromics/FDA IDE](https://paradromics.com/news/paradromics-receives-fda-approval-for-the-connect-one-clinical-study-with-the-connexus-brain-computer-interface/)). Connexus is a fully implanted, wireless high-density array — micro-electrodes "thinner than a human hair" recording single-neuron activity, relayed through an implanted chest receiver, with a preclinical information-transfer rate the company puts at **200+ bits per second**.

Why it matters: the intracortical implant category now has a credible second mover reaching patients, not just Neuralink. That pressures Neuralink's first-mover narrative directly and reframes Synchron's lower-bandwidth endovascular approach as the conservative option rather than the default. The timing compounds the effect — two days earlier, on June 15, a UC Davis group reported in *Nature Medicine* that a single cortical implant let an ALS participant use a speech-and-cursor BCI **independently at home for roughly two years** (see Talent). Read together, the period marks the field crossing from supervised single-lab demonstrations to durable, multi-entrant clinical reality — the precondition investors have been underwriting, and the backdrop to the ~$200M in private rounds below. **[editorial]** The strategic question for BD teams is no longer "does high-bandwidth cortical BCI work in humans" but "which array-and-wireless stack becomes the reference design," and Paradromics just put a stake in that ground.

## 4 · Regulatory

**Wave Neuroscience — MeRT System (EEG-guided TMS), 510(k), Jun 11, for PTSD.** Individualized TMS keyed to each patient's EEG/ECG, cleared via an unconventional 510(k) route rather than De Novo. *Strategic read:* **[editorial]** psychiatry now has an FDA-cleared, biomarker-personalized TMS protocol for PTSD — TMS clinics get a differentiated, higher-margin indication, and the "individualized-stimulation" framing becomes a competitive wedge against generic rTMS ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wave-neuroscience-announces-fda-clearance-of-mert-for-ptsd-302797513.html); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/wave-neuroscience-wins-fda-clearance-for-precision-ptsd-therapy/)).

**Bioness Medical — PoNS (translingual neuromodulation), 510(k), Jun 29, for chronic-stroke gait deficit.** Expands PoNS beyond its prior MS indication; the program was acquired from Helius Medical Technologies. Supporting data: 56.1% responders on active PoNS vs 11.1% controls, as an adjunct to supervised exercise, in patients 22+. *Strategic read:* **[editorial]** with ~7M Americans living with stroke-related gait disability, this is a large-population rehab indication — stroke rehab centers are the immediate channel, and it validates cranial-nerve (tongue) stimulation as a delivery route worth watching ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-clears-pons-device-stroke-rehabilitation-expanding-neurostimulation-beyond-ms-indication); [Practical Neurology](https://practicalneurology.com/news/translingual-neuromodulation-device-receives-fda-510k-clearance-for-stroke-related-gait-deficit/2487643/)).

**Earlier-2026 clearances still shaping the board.** **Flow Neuroscience FL-100** — first at-home prescription tDCS for moderate-to-severe MDD, off a Breakthrough Device Designation and a 174-participant sham-controlled RCT; US availability Q2 ([Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/fda-clears-first-at-home-brain-stimulation-device-for-major-depression)). **Naox "Naox Link"** — first FDA-cleared in-ear EEG, 510(k) Jan 6 ([Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-approves-first-in-ear-eeg-device-expanding-real-world-brain-monitoring-beyond-the-clinic)). *Strategic read:* **[editorial]** home psychiatry neuromodulation (Flow) and gel-free ambulatory EEG (Naox) are the two form-factor shifts most likely to spawn predicate-citing follow-ons this year.

**[editorial] Context, not this period:** Precision Neuroscience's Layer 7 cortical interface — often cited as the reference cortical-electrode clearance — was actually cleared in **April 2025** (510(k), up-to-30-day implantation), so treat it as predecessor context rather than a 2026 action ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/precision-neuroscience-brain-implant-fda-cleared/745913/); [GlobeNewswire, 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/04/17/3063418/0/en/Precision-Neuroscience-Receives-FDA-Clearance-for-High-Resolution-Cortical-Electrode-Array.html)).

## 5 · Talent

*This is the section no competing letter has: the thesis pipeline surfaces who just finished (or is finishing) a PhD in scope — implantable BCIs, speech neuroprostheses, ultrasound neuromodulation, soft bioelectronics — with author, institution, and a hardware/software flag, framed as "who just entered the field." In this hand-built draft, the pipeline output is stood in for by the period's landmark authorship. **[editorial]**, pending live thesis ingest.*

The period's defining result — and its best talent signal — is the *Nature Medicine* paper "**Long-term independent use of an intracortical brain–computer interface for speech and cursor control**" (online Jun 15, print Jul 2026). A 45-year-old ALS participant used a single speech-motor-cortex implant **unsupervised at home for ~19 months**: 3,800+ hours of use, 183,060 sentences / ~1.96M words, 56 words per minute, and 99.2% word accuracy against a 125,000-word vocabulary ([Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04414-6)).

**Who to track from the byline** (the people, not just the institution): first authors **Nicholas S. Card** and **Tyler Singer-Clark**, and **Maitreyee Wairagkar**, working under **Sergey D. Stavisky** and **David M. Brandman** in the **UC Davis Neuroprosthetics Lab**, with **Leigh R. Hochberg** (BrainGate / MGH / Brown / Providence VA) as senior collaborator. The lab-to-industry bridge is explicit this period: **Brandman is also a named investigator on Paradromics' Connect-One study** — the same UC Davis clinical infrastructure sits behind both the academic milestone and the marquee commercial implant. **[editorial]** For the quarterly aggregate view, the labs to watch as talent sources remain UC Davis (Stavisky/Brandman), UCSF (Chang), the Stanford neuroprosthetics lineage, and the BrainGate consortium — the same rosters that startups and the implant companies are recruiting from.
